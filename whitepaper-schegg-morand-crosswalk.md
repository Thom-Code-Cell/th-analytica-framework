# Schegg/Morand Whitepaper Requirements Crosswalk

Source: Morand & Schegg, *Werden Sie für KI sichtbar*, v0.4.6, 3 February 2026.

This crosswalk records how source themes are incorporated without treating the Whitepaper as an AI ranking specification.

| Whitepaper theme | TH Analytica control | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Clear hotel entity | Canonical entity and property identity | Incorporated |
| Property, room and service attributes | Hospitality Attribute Inventory | Expanded with ownership, provenance and freshness |
| Website and JSON-LD | Structured representation | Incorporated; no recommendation guarantee |
| Website, OTA, GBP and TIS consistency | Cross-surface consistency | Expanded to field-level conflict evidence |
| Management, operations and webmaster roles | Freshness and operating process | Incorporated as accountable roles |
| TIS/DMO and national data hubs | Tourism data ecosystem | Incorporated with applicability by market |
| Reviews and external signals | Reputation and external corroboration | Incorporated with sample/time-window controls |
| LLM robot access | Technical Foundation and Agent Readiness | Already covered; crawler functions remain distinct |
| AI agents and booking | Agent and transaction readiness | Expanded with security, authorisation and live testing |
| Physical venues and AI glasses | Physical AI Governance | TH Analytica extension beyond the source scope |

## Guardrails

- Structured data supports explicit machine-readable meaning; it does not prove crawling, retrieval, citation, selection or recommendation.
- Simulated prompts are examples, not causal evidence.
- Third-party metrics retain their original scope and may not be generalised to every hotel.
- `robots.txt`, `ai.txt` and `llms.txt` have different support and effects; none is a universal control plane.
- Dynamic price and availability claims require authoritative, time-bounded evidence.
