# TH Analytica Privacy-by-Design Standard

Status: **Mandatory framework control**  
Effective: 2026-09-16

## Principle

Every TH Analytica analysis and every generated customer-facing or machine-readable file must apply privacy by design and privacy-friendly defaults. The applicable jurisdictions and sector rules must be identified for the concrete project. This standard is a control framework and not a substitute for case-specific legal advice.

## Capture default

Photo, video and audio capture involving an identifiable person is **prohibited by default**. It may be enabled only where the responsible organisation has documented:

1. a specific and legitimate purpose;
2. an applicable legal basis;
3. necessity and proportionality;
4. the required transparency and notice;
5. consent where consent is the chosen or required legal basis;
6. the shortest justified retention period and a deletion process;
7. access, security, processor and cross-border-transfer controls;
8. a process for data-subject requests, objections and withdrawal where applicable.

If the legal basis, authority, notice or scope is missing or unclear, the system must stop the capture or escalate to an accountable human. Silence, entry into a venue or merely seeing a sign must not automatically be treated as valid consent.

## Enhanced protection

Biometric identification, emotion inference, health-related inference, tracking across zones, and processing involving children, patients or other vulnerable persons are prohibited by default and require a separately documented high-risk assessment, explicit organisational approval and any legally required data-protection impact assessment or legal review.

Private rooms, treatment areas, sanitary areas, changing rooms, classrooms and comparable sensitive zones must not expose exact coordinates or enable capture by default.

## Mandatory analysis record

Every analysis records:

- applicable jurisdiction and responsible controller;
- categories of personal data and affected persons;
- purpose and legal basis for each processing operation;
- consent mechanism where relied upon, including withdrawal;
- minimisation, on-device processing and anonymisation options;
- retention, deletion and secondary-use restrictions;
- transparency and data-subject-rights process;
- processors, subprocessors and international transfers;
- security controls and incident route;
- high-risk/DPIA decision;
- outcome: **approved**, **approved with conditions**, **prohibited**, or **open for legal review**.

An **open** state blocks implementation and publication of an enabling policy.

## File-generation rule

Generated policies, notices, schemas and recommendations must not invent consent, a legal basis, retention periods or regulatory approval. Unknown facts remain explicitly open. Public files contain only the minimum information needed for their purpose and never publish sensitive indoor coordinates or personal contact data unless publication is necessary, authorised and documented.

## Legal accuracy

Consent is not presented as the only possible legal basis in every jurisdiction. The framework nevertheless uses the stricter operational default above: no identifiable capture until its lawfulness and safeguards have been documented. The responsible organisation remains accountable for the final legal assessment and implementation.
